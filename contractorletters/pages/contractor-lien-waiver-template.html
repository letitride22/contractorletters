The 4 Types of Lien Waivers

There are four standard lien waiver forms used across the industry. Knowing which one to sign — and when — is critical to protecting your payment rights.

01
Conditional Waiver on Progress Payment
Waives lien rights for work completed to date — but ONLY becomes effective when the specified payment actually clears.
✓ Safe to sign before payment clears
02
Unconditional Waiver on Progress Payment
Permanently waives lien rights for work completed to date. Effective immediately upon signing regardless of payment status.
⚠️ Only sign after payment fully clears
03
Conditional Waiver on Final Payment
Waives ALL lien rights on the entire project — but only becomes effective when final payment clears.
✓ Safe to sign before final payment clears
04
Unconditional Waiver on Final Payment
Permanently waives ALL lien rights on the entire project. Immediately effective. No turning back.
⚠️ Only sign after final payment fully clears

⚠️ The golden rule: Never sign an unconditional waiver before payment clears your bank. A check in hand is not a cleared payment. Wire transfers clear same day; checks take 2-5 business days. Signing an unconditional waiver on a bounced check means you've permanently given up your lien rights for nothing.

What Every Lien Waiver Must Include

  • ✓Claimant name — your name or company name as the contractor waiving rights
  • ✓Property owner name — the person who owns the property where work was done
  • ✓Property address — the job site address
  • ✓Through date — the date through which you're waiving rights (for progress payments)
  • ✓Payment amount — the exact dollar amount of the payment this waiver covers
  • ✓Waiver type — clearly state whether conditional or unconditional
  • ✓Signature and date — authorized representative of the contracting company

State-Specific Lien Waiver Rules

Several states have mandatory statutory lien waiver forms — using a non-compliant form can render the waiver invalid. The strictest states include California, Texas, Arizona, Nevada, and Mississippi, all of which have specific required language or forms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a GC require me to sign a lien waiver before paying me?

Yes, this is standard practice — but they should only ask for a conditional waiver, not an unconditional one. A conditional waiver protects both parties: you get paid and they get assurance the lien rights are waived once payment clears. If a GC demands an unconditional waiver before payment, that's a red flag.

Does signing a lien waiver affect my right to sue for payment?

A lien waiver only waives your mechanics lien rights — not your right to sue for breach of contract. You can still take legal action even after signing a lien waiver. However, the lien is often your most powerful leverage, so waiving it prematurely is still a significant concession.

What's the difference between a lien waiver and a lien release?

A lien waiver is signed before or at the time of payment and waives your right to file a lien in exchange for payment. A lien release is signed after a lien has already been filed and releases that specific lien from the property title. They serve different purposes at different stages.

Do subcontractors need separate lien waivers?

Yes — each party in the payment chain (GC, sub, sub-sub, supplier) has independent lien rights and must sign their own waiver. As a GC, getting lien waivers from your subs and suppliers when you pay them protects you from having a sub file a lien on a project you've already paid for.
