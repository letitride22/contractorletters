What is a Change Order?

A change order is a written amendment to the original contract that documents any work added to, removed from, or modified from the original scope. It specifies what changed, why, the cost impact, and the timeline impact. Both parties sign it before the additional work begins.

Without a signed change order, extra work is essentially a gift. Courts consistently rule in favor of contractors who have documented change orders versus those who claim verbal agreements.

What a Change Order Must Include

  • ✓Change order number — track them sequentially (CO-001, CO-002)
  • ✓Reference to original contract — date and job address
  • ✓Detailed description of the change — what is being added, removed, or modified
  • ✓Reason for the change — client request, hidden condition, code requirement
  • ✓Cost impact — original contract amount, change order amount, new total
  • ✓Schedule impact — any days added to the project timeline
  • ✓Both signatures — contractor AND client before work begins

⚠️ Never start extra work without a signed change order. "They said it was fine" is not a legal agreement. A verbal okay costs you nothing to document in writing — and potentially thousands if you don't.

Smart practice: Number your change orders sequentially and keep copies. When billing the final invoice, list every change order by number. This makes your billing transparent and nearly impossible to dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the client refuses to sign a change order but wants the work done?

Stop work on the additional scope until they sign. Politely explain that you can't proceed with extra work without written authorization — it's standard practice, not a personal issue. If they refuse and you proceed anyway, you've essentially agreed to do it for free.

Can I charge for change orders on a fixed-price contract?

Absolutely — a fixed price covers the original scope only. Any work outside that scope is billable. Your contract should include a change order clause making this clear. If it doesn't, add one to all future contracts.

How quickly should I submit a change order?

As soon as you identify the scope change — ideally before starting the extra work. The longer you wait, the harder it is to get approval. Clients also tend to "forget" verbal agreements over time. Same-day documentation is the standard.

What if the change is small — do I still need a formal change order?

Yes, for anything that affects cost or schedule. Small changes add up fast. A $200 change order today and a $150 one next week becomes $1,400 at the end of a project. Clients are more likely to dispute a large undocumented bill than a series of small signed change orders.
