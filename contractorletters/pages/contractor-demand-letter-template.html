Why a Demand Letter Works

Most non-paying clients aren't malicious — they're disorganized, cash-strapped, or testing whether you'll actually follow through. A formal written demand letter signals that you're serious, you know your rights, and you're prepared to escalate. Studies consistently show that 70-80% of unpaid invoices are resolved after a formal demand letter — before any legal action is needed.

Beyond psychology, a demand letter creates a paper trail. If you end up filing a lien or suing in small claims court, a judge will want to see that you gave the client reasonable written notice and opportunity to pay.

What a Contractor Demand Letter Must Include

  • ✓Client name and address — full legal name of the person or entity that owes you money
  • ✓Job description and address — what work was performed and where
  • ✓Invoice number and original due date — reference your paper trail
  • ✓Exact amount owed — principal plus any contractual late fees
  • ✓Payment deadline — give 7-14 days, firm but reasonable
  • ✓Consequences of non-payment — mention lien rights, small claims, collections
  • ✓Your signature — as contractor or company owner

Lien Rights: Your Most Powerful Tool

Every state gives contractors, subcontractors, and suppliers the right to file a mechanics lien against a property where work was performed. A lien clouds the property title — meaning the owner can't sell or refinance until the lien is resolved. Mentioning your lien rights in a demand letter gets attention fast.

⚠️ Lien deadlines are strict and vary by state. Most states require you to file within 60-90 days of your last day of work. Missing the deadline permanently waives your lien rights. If payment is seriously overdue, don't wait — file a preliminary lien notice now while you still can.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I demand — just the invoice or can I add late fees?

You can include late fees if your contract or invoice specifically stated a late fee policy. If you never mentioned late fees in writing, it's risky to add them now — stick to the original invoice amount. Going forward, always include a late fee clause in every contract.

Should I threaten a lawsuit in the demand letter?

Mention your legal options (lien, small claims, collections) without making it sound like a personal threat. Phrases like "we will pursue all available legal remedies" are professional and effective. Avoid emotional language — it weakens your position.

What if the client disputes the amount owed?

Send the demand letter anyway — it forces them to respond in writing with their objection. A written dispute is far better than silence. You can then negotiate or escalate based on their response. If you have a signed contract and completion photos, you're in a strong position.

How long should I give them to pay?

7-14 days is standard. Shorter feels aggressive and may not give them time to process payment. Longer loses urgency. Ten days is a solid middle ground — firm enough to signal seriousness, fair enough to hold up well if the matter goes to court.
