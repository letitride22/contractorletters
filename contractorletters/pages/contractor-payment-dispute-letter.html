How to Write an Effective Dispute Letter

The goal of a payment dispute letter is to clearly state what you're owed, why you're owed it, and what you expect to happen next — in a professional tone that holds up in court if needed.

  • 1State the facts clearly — amount paid, amount owed, the difference, and the date payment was due
  • 2Reference your documentation — contract date, invoice number, change order numbers, any written approvals
  • 3Dispute the deduction specifically — if they deducted for alleged defects, state clearly that the work meets contract specifications
  • 4State your demand — exact amount owed, deadline for payment
  • 5Note your remedies — mention lien rights, prompt payment act penalties, and legal action without being threatening

Prompt Payment Acts: Most states have Prompt Payment Acts that require GCs and owners to pay contractors within a specific timeframe (often 7-30 days of invoice approval). Violations can result in interest penalties of 1-2% per month automatically. Mentioning the applicable Prompt Payment Act in your dispute letter adds serious legal weight.

Retainage Disputes

Retainage — typically 5-10% of each payment held until project completion — is one of the most common dispute sources. Once a project is substantially complete, most states require retainage to be released within a specific timeframe. If a client is holding retainage beyond that period without a valid documented deficiency list, they may be violating state law.

Key retainage facts: California limits retainage to 5% on most projects. Texas requires release within 30 days of substantial completion. Federal projects have specific retainage reduction requirements. Know your state's rules before sending the dispute letter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the client claims the work was defective to justify the deduction?

Request the deficiency claim in writing immediately. A vague verbal claim of "bad work" doesn't justify withholding payment. Once you have it in writing, you can respond point by point. Document your work with photos, inspection reports, and any third-party approvals you received.

Can I charge interest on the disputed amount?

Yes, if your contract includes an interest clause or your state's Prompt Payment Act applies. Typical contractual late interest runs 1.5-2% per month. Some state Prompt Payment Acts automatically trigger interest without a contract clause. Include the interest amount in your demand.

Should I accept a partial payment while disputing the rest?

Be careful — accepting a check marked "payment in full" can constitute accord and satisfaction, potentially waiving your right to the remaining balance. Write "accepted under protest, not payment in full" on the endorsement, or better yet, consult an attorney before cashing any disputed partial payment check.

When should I escalate to a lien or small claims court?

If the client doesn't respond to your dispute letter within your stated deadline (7-14 days), escalate immediately. File a lien if you're still within the lien deadline for your state. For amounts under $10,000-$25,000 (varies by state), small claims court is fast, cheap, and doesn't require a lawyer.
